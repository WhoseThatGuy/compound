Five Zones, Nine Platforms

What's actually
on the floor.

No generic "fully equipped gym" copy. Compound's floor is laid out in 5 zones — here's exactly what's in each one, so you know what you're training on before you show up.

The Floor, Zone By Zone

Five zones,
one training floor.

Zone 1 — Warmup

Get moving before you load the bar. First stop on the floor, before you get into the rest of the zones.

Zone 2 — Upper Body

Dual-stack cable stations, a full run of selectorized machines, and a full dumbbell range — pressing, pulling, and accessory work for everything above the waist.

Zone 3 — Lower Body

Nine lifting platforms with Eleiko competition bars and specialty bars, Industrial Athletic bumper plates colour-coded and calibrated in kilograms, plus a 45-degree leg press and plate-loaded machines. The core of the floor.

Zone 4 — Full Body

Kettlebells and compound movement space for training that doesn't split neatly into upper or lower body.

Zone 5 — Performance

40m of indoor turf for sprints, sled pushes and pulls, rowing ergs, and air bikes — the aerobic and conditioning end of the floor.

How We Train

What it's actually
like to train here.

Five national-level athletes train on this floor. So do people in their first week. Nobody's watching to judge where you're starting from, and it's not a flexing-in-the-mirror kind of place. Read the Google reviews and you'll get the same read we just gave you.

Can I drop the weights?

Yes. Deadlifts, cleans, anything better dropped than babied down.

"I was able to do olympic lifting training. A lot of gyms don't allow you to drop weights as part of…" — Reno Paotonu, Google review

Do I need shoes on?

No — barefoot and socks-only training is fine.
